> **Opening question:** Which absences are necessary simplifications, and which change the map's meaning or fairness?

## Planned learning sequence

1. List what is visibly present.
2. Identify missing places, people, time periods, categories, uncertainty, and lived experiences.
3. Trace each omission to data collection, classification, scale, interface, privacy, or institutional purpose.
4. Decide which omissions require disclosure, additional evidence, redesign, or a different map.
5. Draft a concise limitations statement.

## Critical pause

Whose absence is most likely to be mistaken for nonexistence, irrelevance, or lack of need?

## Planned checkpoint

Learners identify three different omissions and propose one responsible response to each.
