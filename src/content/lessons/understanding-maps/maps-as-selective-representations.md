> **Opening question:** How can two accurate maps of the same place communicate different worlds?

## Planned learning sequence

1. Compare two maps without reading their captions.
2. Inventory what each includes, groups, emphasizes, and omits.
3. Introduce selection, simplification, classification, scale, and framing.
4. Connect each choice to audience and purpose.
5. Revise one map for a different intended user.

## Critical pause

Who decides which features deserve to become visible, named, bounded, or measurable?

## Planned checkpoint

Learners annotate a map with at least four representational choices and explain how one alternative choice could change its meaning.
