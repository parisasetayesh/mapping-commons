> **Opening question:** What must your map help someone understand, compare, locate, or decide?

## Planned learning sequence

1. Separate the broad topic from the intended decision or inquiry.
2. Name the phenomenon, population, place, time period, and audience.
3. Identify whether the question requires locations, areas, routes, relationships, or change over time.
4. List the evidence and comparison required.
5. Test whether a map adds information that a table, chart, narrative, or photograph would not.

## Critical pause

Does framing the issue as a spatial question clarify it, or does it risk making structural, historical, or nonspatial relationships disappear?

## Planned checkpoint

Learners complete: “How does **[phenomenon]** vary across **[place or geography]** during **[time]**, for **[audience and purpose]**?”
